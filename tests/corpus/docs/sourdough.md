# Sourdough

A control document. Nothing here relates to the service; a lexical query about
code must never surface it, and a paraphrased query about baking must.

## Starter

Mix equal weights of flour and water and leave it at room temperature. Discard
half and refeed daily. After about a week it doubles within four hours of a
feeding, smells sharply sour, and is ready to leaven bread. A starter kept in the
refrigerator needs feeding only weekly, but wants two days of room-temperature
refeeding before you bake with it.

## Mixing

Combine flour and water and rest for an hour before adding the levain. This
autolyse lets the flour hydrate fully, which makes the dough noticeably easier to
handle later. Add salt with the levain, not before — salt tightens the gluten and
slows fermentation.

## Bulk fermentation

Four to five hours at 24°C, with a fold every forty-five minutes for the first
three. The dough is ready when it has risen by half, feels airy, and holds the
shape of a fold rather than slumping back immediately. Watch the dough, not the
clock: a warm kitchen can cut an hour off this, and a cold one can add two.

## Shaping and proof

Pre-shape, rest twenty minutes, then shape tightly and move to a banneton.
Refrigerate overnight — the long cold proof develops the acidity that gives the
loaf its flavor and makes the dough firm enough to score cleanly.

## Baking

Preheat a Dutch oven to 250°C. Bake covered for twenty minutes, uncovered for
another twenty-five at 230°C. The crust should be dark, almost mahogany;
underbaked sourdough tastes flat no matter how well it fermented. Cool
completely on a rack before slicing, or the crumb turns gummy.
